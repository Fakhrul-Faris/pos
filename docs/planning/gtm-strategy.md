## 1. Executive summary

Miki is a **bootstrapped, 2-founder, part-time** company targeting **100 paying merchants in year 1**. We have **no paid marketing budget**. Malaysia is the focus for months 1–6; international expansion begins months 7–12.

We do not win by out-spending **Fresha** or out-featuring incumbents. We win the way underdogs like **Figma, Notion, and Cal.com** did:

1. **Change what the fight is about** — wedge on queue + booking for chairs, BYOD, no hardware.
2. **Bake distribution into the product** — every customer booking touchpoint is a Miki impression.
3. **Sell bottom-up to the person who feels the pain** — the shop owner, not a procurement team.
4. **Let users and creators market for us** — templates, barber TikTokers, referral credits.
5. **Automate everything that isn't product** — onboarding emails, SEO pages, free tools.

**North-star metric (year 1):** 100 paying merchants  
**Leading metric (months 1–6):** Trial → first real customer booking within 7 days  
**Category claim:** *Flat subscription. Your queue works. Your money stays yours.*  
**Primary competitor (barbershop):** [Fresha](https://www.fresha.com) — see §6.1.

## 4. The underdog framework

Every major underdog win follows the same loop:

```
Pain giant ignores
    → Narrow wedge product
        → Individual adopts alone (bottom-up)
            → Product exposes others (viral surface)
                → Community / creators amplify
                    → More individuals adopt
```

### What underdogs do NOT do early

| Skip | Why |
| :--- | :--- |
| Feature parity with incumbent | You lose on resources |
| Enterprise sales motion | Too slow, too expensive |
| Brand ad campaigns | No budget; no awareness to harvest |
| Broad horizontal positioning | "POS for everyone" = POS for no one |
| Paid acquisition at scale | CAC kills bootstrapped SaaS |

### The seven universal moves

| # | Move | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **Wedge, not parity** | One job done impossibly well that the giant structurally can't copy |
| 2 | **Distribution in product** | Sharing, embeds, powered-by — the product is the ad |
| 3 | **Bottom-up adoption** | Win the daily user; they bring the org |
| 4 | **Infiltrate existing communities** | Go where users already gather; don't build your own day one |
| 5 | **Users create your content** | Templates, tutorials, UGC — you amplify, not produce everything |
| 6 | **Contrarian narrative** | Reframe the category; punch identity, not spec sheets |
| 7 | **Delay expensive GTM** | No sales team, no ads, until self-serve converts without humans |

## 5. Case studies — what to steal

### 5.1 Figma vs Adobe

**Incumbent optimized for:** Desktop apps, individual designer productivity, file email chains, enterprise licenses.

**Figma's wedge:** Real-time collaboration in the browser — a pain Adobe couldn't fix without cannibalizing its desktop business.

| Early tactic | What they did | Miki parallel |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Stealth community planting | Built 1:1 relationships in *existing* design communities (Twitter, meetups) before launch | Lurk in barber FB groups, r/barber — help first, mention second |
| Designer advocates | Hired passionate designers to create content and feedback loops — not traditional marketers | Barber micro-influencers (free months, not salary) |
| Viral by design | Share a Figma link → anyone views/edits/comments | Customer booking link + status page → anyone sees Miki |
| Free tier as distribution | Viewers become users | 14-day full trial → Lite keeps QR live (loss aversion) |
| Bottom-up | Individual designer → team → company. **No sales team ~4 years after launch** | Owner adopts alone → tells barber friend → same street |
| Contrarian story | "Design tool in the browser" sounded wrong until it wasn't | "POS tanpa mesin" — BYOD, live this afternoon |

**Gut punch:** Adobe sold tools for designers. Figma sold workflow for everyone around design. ~67% of Figma growth came from non-designers.

**Miki lesson:** Don't beat Fresha on booking polish or marketplace discovery. Beat them on **Saturday queue workflow + Malaysia-native rails** — walk-ins + bookings on one counter, DuitNow, predictable pricing, no commission trap.

**Sources:** [First Round — Figma community-led growth](https://review.firstround.com/the-5-phases-of-figmas-community-led-growth-from-stealth-to-enterprise/) · [First Round podcast — Claire Butler](https://review.firstround.com/podcast/the-5-phases-of-figmas-community-led-growth-claire-butler/)

### 5.2 Notion vs Evernote / Confluence

**Incumbent optimized for:** Note capture (Evernote), enterprise wiki (Confluence) — utilitarian, not shareable.

**Notion's wedge:** Flexible blocks + templates — workspace as identity and content.

| Early tactic | What they did | Miki parallel |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Super-users before staff | Ben Lang built Notion Pages (template hub) before being hired; Notion amplified | Shop owners who film "switch from WhatsApp" — amplify their UGC |
| Ambassador program | One tweet → 1,000 applications; early access + event budget, not cash | "Founding barber" program — RM89 locked, referral credits |
| Template gallery | Community solves onboarding; every template = SEO + shareable asset | Pre-built shop templates (fade shop, 2-chair salon) — setup in 10 min |
| YouTube army | Creators made tutorials because Notion was content-worthy | Barber TikTok / setup tutorials — give them demo + affiliate code |
| Let go of control | Supported unofficial subreddits, FB groups, fan meetups | Don't police fan content; provide brand kit + guidelines |
| Aesthetic as moat | "My Notion setup" posts went viral | "My shop booking page" — clean QR, professional status page |

**Gut punch:** Evernote sold storage. Notion sold *"look at my life system."* The product became lifestyle content.

**Miki lesson:** Merchants won't post "my POS setup" for aesthetics — but they will share **"I stopped WhatsApp booking chaos"** and show a clean booking page to customers.

**Sources:** [First Round — How Notion does marketing](https://review.firstround.com/how-notion-does-marketing-a-deep-dive-into-its-community-influencers-growth-playbooks/) · [Ben Lang — Advocacy at Notion](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/best-practices-from-scaling-effective-advocacy-program-ben-lang-vkzff)

### 5.3 Cal.com vs Calendly

**Incumbent optimized for:** Closed SaaS, polished scheduling, walled garden.

**Cal.com's wedge:** Scheduling as open infrastructure — forkable, embeddable, "Calendly alternative" SEO.

| Early tactic | What they did | Miki parallel |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Open source as GTM | GitHub stars = trust + community + contributors | Not applicable at launch — substitute with transparency (build in public, open roadmap) |
| Product Hunt ignition | Alpha launch → #1 Product of Day/Week/Month; ~50 paying day one | One coordinated PH launch when billing + onboarding are bulletproof (month 4+) |
| Every link = marketing | Avg user shares scheduling link ~250 times | Every customer QR scan + booking page = Miki touchpoint |
| SEO wedge | Owned "Calendly open source alternative" | Own **"Fresha alternative Malaysia"**, "Fresha alternative barbershop", "pos barbershop tanpa komisen" |
| Build in public | Open metrics, public roadmap, founder Twitter | Weekly ship log — Ayol on LinkedIn/X, BM + EN |
| Re-launch moments | Major version + funding = new PH spike | Vertical waitlist opens (clinic, café) = new launch moments |

**Gut punch:** Calendly sold convenience. Cal.com sold ownership — *your scheduling layer, not rented.*

**Miki lesson:** Own the phrase **"your shop's queue layer — flat price, no commission."** Cal.com beat Calendly with "open scheduling"; Miki beats Fresha with **predictable SaaS vs revenue tax.**

**Sources:** [Cal.com Series A memo](https://cal.com/blog/series-a-memo) · [Product Hunt — Cal.com launch story](https://www.producthunt.com/stories/how-this-open-source-calendly-alternative-rocketed-to-product-of-the-day)

## 6. Competitive landscape & positioning

### 6.1 Primary competitor — Fresha (barbershop)

**Who they are:** UK-born global salon/barbershop booking platform. Strong brand with barbers and salons worldwide. The tool most Malaysian barbers already know or try first.

**What Fresha optimized for:**
- Beautiful online booking UX and client discovery marketplace
- Card-centric payments (Stripe) — revenue from processing + subscriptions
- Salon/spa scheduling — appointments first, walk-ins second
- Global scale — one product for every country

**What barbers complain about (2025–2026):**
| Pain | Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Subscription already 2× Miki** | USD 14.95/bookable member → **~RM200/mo for 3 barbers** vs Miki Ocelot **RM109** (4 barbers) |
| **Revenue tax on top** | **20%** marketplace commission on new clients + **~2.29% + RM1** card processing per txn |
| **Bill unpredictability** | Busy month = higher fees; Miki flat SaaS unless merchant opts into HitPay |
| **Not free anymore** | Permanent free plan removed early 2025; ~7-day trial |
| **Weak walk-in queue** | Appointment-first; Saturday walk-in rush is an afterthought |
| **No Malaysia-native payments** | Card/Stripe-first; no native **DuitNow QR** (dominant in MY shops) |
| **No LHDN e-Invoice** | Merchants issue MyInvois manually on the side — extra workflow, compliance risk |
| **No WhatsApp-native ops** | MY shops run on WhatsApp; Fresha is in-app/email/SMS (metered) |
| **Per-barber economics weak** | Revenue/cut tracking for chair-rental barbers not a core strength |
| **Global = generic** | Support, tax, and payment rails don't fit Malaysian SME reality |

**Verified pricing (Malaysia enquiry, July 2026):** 3 barbers = USD 44.85/mo (~RM195–210) base · 20% marketplace · 2.29% + RM1 card. Full worked example: [`compare.md`](../modules/barbershop/compare.md) §Cost framing.

**Business model verdict:** Fresha monetises **through your revenue**. Miki monetises **through flat subscription**. On maths alone, a 3-chair shop can save **~RM1,000+/year** on subscription — and **RM5,000–10,000+/year** once processing and commission stack. UI polish does not close that gap.

**Product craft:** Fresha's booking UX is good but generic (salon/spa/nails globally). Miki is **barbershop-only** — queue + counter + booking as one system. A focused vertical tool can beat a global app's UX where it matters (Saturday counter, per-barber cuts, DuitNow). **Lead with business model in marketing; let product design remove the last objection on trial.**

**Sources:** [Zenoti — Best barbershop software 2026](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/best-barbershop-software-2026) · [LaunchIt — Fresha alternative Malaysia](https://www.launchit.my/en/blog/fresha-alternative-malaysia) · [RZRV — Fresha fees explained](https://rzrv.ai/blog/fresha-fees-explained) · Fresha pricing enquiry (founders, July 2026)

## 7. Viral loop spec

This is Miki's highest-leverage GTM asset — the Cal.com lesson in product form. **Every customer-facing surface is a distribution channel.**

### 7.1 Loop diagram

```
Merchant signs up (self-serve trial)
    → Sets up shop + prints QR (< 20 min)
        → Puts QR in window / IG bio / WhatsApp status
            → Customer scans → books / joins queue
                → Customer sees booking status page (Powered by Miki)
                    → Other merchants / customers ask "what app is that?"
                        → Merchant refers OR customer asks their barber
                            → New merchant signs up
```

### 7.2 Touchpoints (what people see)

| Surface | Who sees it | Branding | CTA for observer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Shop QR poster** | Walk-by customers | Miki logo + shop name | Scan to book |
| **Customer booking page** | Customer (phone) | Subtle footer: *Queue & booking by Miki* | — |
| **Queue status page** | Customer waiting | *Powered by Miki* + short link | "Own a shop? Get yours" |
| **Digital receipt / SMS link** | Customer post-visit | Miki footer on receipt page | — |
| **Merchant booking link** | IG bio visitors, WhatsApp shares | Branded booking URL (`book.miki.my/{shop}`) | Book appointment |
| **Queue board (TV/tablet)** | In-shop customers | Optional small Miki mark | — |

### 7.3 Branding rules

| Rule | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- |
| Footer on customer pages — never intrusive | Cal.com puts branding on every link; we do the same, lightly |
| Customer flow never blocked by branding | Trust > extraction; don't annoy end customers |
| Merchant can hide branding on **Patriot+** (future) | Upsell path; default = powered-by on Lite/Ocelot |
| QR poster generator includes Miki mark | Printable asset = permanent in-shop billboard |

### 7.4 Activation = marketing

**Aha moment:** Merchant gets first real customer booking within 7 days of signup.

| Step | Target time | Automated nudge |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Signup → shop created | < 2 min | Pre-loaded demo menu (fade shop template) |
| Shop → QR downloaded | < 15 min | In-app checklist + PDF download |
| QR → first booking | < 7 days | Day 1 email: "Paste link in IG bio"; Day 3: "Add a walk-in" |
| Trial day 10 | — | Founding RM89 offer email |
| Trial day 14 | — | Loss-aversion: barber 3 + calendar removed on Lite |
| Post-pay | — | Referral prompt: 1 month free per paying referral |

See [`../modules/barbershop/pricing-funnel.md`](../modules/barbershop/pricing-funnel.md) for trial → Lite → paid mechanics.

### 7.5 Referral loop (merchant → merchant)

| Trigger | Action | Reward |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Day of first paid month | In-app: "Know another shop?" | — |
| Referee signs up + pays 1 month | Auto bill credit | Referrer: **1 month free** ([`phase1-plan.md`](phase1-plan.md) B-07) |
| Founding barber shares code | Track via referral code / UTM | Both get extended trial or credit |

**Barber-to-barber is the Figma "share file link" equivalent** — highest-trust channel in the wedge.

### 7.6 Creator loop (merchant → audience → merchant)

| Actor | Action | Miki support |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Barber TikToker (5k–50k) | Films "why I switched from Fresha" / "Fresha fees explained" | Free months + affiliate code; comparison one-pager |
| Shop owner (UGC) | Posts booking page / Saturday rush clip | Repost from @miki; founding slot shoutout |

## 15. AI / AEO marketing (summary)

> **Full playbook:** [`aeo-strategy.md`](aeo-strategy.md) · **Monthly audit:** [`aeo-audit-template.md`](aeo-audit-template.md) · **Deploy:** [`llms.txt`](llms.txt) → `https://miki.my/llms.txt`

AI-focused marketing for Miki = **Answer Engine Optimisation (AEO)** — get cited when barbers ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, or Google AI Overview about Fresha alternatives, barbershop booking, or fee stacks.

### Why it fits Miki

| Fit | Reason |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bootstrapped + part-time | 5 pages + 30 min/month audit — no content team |
| Fresha fee research | Concrete numbers AI extracts and quotes |
| `/compare` already built | Tables + FAQ + worked example = citation-ready |
| High-intent queries | "Fresha alternative Malaysia" = close to trial |

### Discovery path

```
AI answer names/cites Miki
    → branded Google search or click-through
        → /compare or /tools/fresha-cost
            → 14-day trial
```

Optimise for **citation + branded search**, not only AI click-through (zero-click answers are common).

### Three pillars

| Pillar | Miki actions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Structure** | `/compare`, `/barbershop`, calculator — tables, FAQs, 40–60 word answer blocks |
| **Authority** | Cite Fresha pricing sources; freshness dates; founder quote; third-party Reddit/creators |
| **Presence** | `llms.txt`, allow AI crawlers in robots.txt, FAQ schema, SSR pages |

### Minimum content stack (year 1)

1. `/barbershop` · 2. `/compare` · 3. `/tools/fresha-cost` · 4. `/pricing` · 5. `/llms.txt`

### Canonical AI-extractable paragraph

> For a 3-chair Malaysian barbershop, Fresha costs approximately RM195–210/month in base subscription alone (USD 14.95 per bookable staff member), plus 20% commission on marketplace new clients' first booking and roughly 2.29% + RM1.00 per card transaction. Miki Ocelot is RM109/month flat for up to 4 barbers, with RM0 platform fees on cash and own DuitNow QR, and no marketplace commission.

Use on compare page, barbershop page, `llms.txt`, and schema descriptions.

| 7–10 | Fresha calculator + shareable result URLs | Haziq |

| 12 | Re-run audit | Ayol |

| Metric | Target |
| :--- | :--- |
| Tier-1 citation rate | >10% |
| Branded search (GSC) | Uptrend |
